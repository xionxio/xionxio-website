Portable Nebulizer Distributor Evaluation Guide

A distributor programme has to answer more than “what is the price?” It needs a model, market, document, sample and commercial pathway that the purchasing team can inspect in sequence.

Key takeaways

  • Country and buyer type should be declared before documents or claim language are discussed.
  • Distributors should request a model-specific product record, not a mixed catalogue image.
  • Sample review and commercial terms should follow an agreed product/document scope.

1. Define the market and channel before comparing offers.

A portable device intended for an importer, medical distributor, clinic procurement team or private-label brand may require different documentation, labels, instructions and support expectations. The first enquiry should identify the target country, the expected channel and whether the buyer is evaluating a listed product or a branded programme.

This does not require a long regulatory report at first contact. It simply creates the context needed to ask responsible next questions. Without it, public marketing language may be misaligned with what the distributor can actually represent in its market.

2. Ask for a record that belongs to the exact product.

For each model under review, establish the reference, configuration, accessory set and relevant IFU/specification version. When source material lists two models, as it does for ZY018G-I and ZY018G-I-2, the distributor should treat the records separately until an official difference table is available.

The product record should resolve basic questions such as visible structure, charging format, pack-out, final dimensions and product identification. It should not rely on a generic lifestyle image to make a model-level promise.

3. Make the document request suitable for an internal review.

A distributor may need to bring the product to commercial, technical and compliance colleagues. The request should therefore state the model and explain which categories are needed: specification, IFU, labels, packaging, registration scope or quality-related information. The supplier response can then be directed to the actual review purpose.

For a regulated product, documents from one country are not automatically proof of market access in another. A responsible B2B site and supplier conversation explains this boundary rather than hiding it behind broad certification wording.

4. Use samples to answer a defined set of questions.

Before sample evaluation, agree the model, accessory configuration, evaluation purpose, accompanying documents and feedback process. A channel team can then decide whether the product should progress to a commercial programme, further document review or a different configuration discussion.

Sample evaluation should not be used as a shortcut around final specifications or instructions. It supports the buyer’s assessment; it does not replace the product file.

5. Move to commercial terms only after the review path is clear.

MOQ, lead time, shipping, warranty and local service information are commercially important, but none should be invented on a preliminary public page. They should be quoted after the specific model, pack-out, market route and project scope are understood.

This sequence is not slower. It reduces the rework that occurs when an attractive initial quote is based on an unspecified device or unsupported documentation assumption.
