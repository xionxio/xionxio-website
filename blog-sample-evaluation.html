How to Structure a Portable Nebulizer Sample Evaluation

A sample is more useful when it arrives with a named configuration, an evaluation purpose and the right product information. This guide turns a sample request into a controlled procurement step.

Key takeaways

  • A sample should be tied to a named model and configuration.
  • Write down the questions the sample is intended to answer.
  • Use sample feedback to decide the next record or project action; do not treat it as a substitute for final documentation.

1. Define the evaluation scope before the sample moves.

Begin with the model reference, accessory configuration, target channel and buyer role. A distributor may be evaluating portfolio fit; an OEM team may be evaluating whether a private-label path is feasible. Those are different questions and should not be mixed together.

If the supplier’s source material lists multiple models, identify the exact model in the sample request. A generic request for “the portable device in the photo” creates ambiguity in sample pack-out and later document review.

2. Set the questions the sample should answer.

Do not ask the sample to prove every possible claim. Instead, identify the operational and procurement questions your team needs to resolve: product identity, visible construction, accessory format, packaging direction, usability against the applicable instructions, and suitability for a later document review.

Questions about intended use, compatible solutions, safety, material claims and specific technical performance must remain tied to the final documentation and appropriate test evidence. The sample can support an evaluation; it does not independently approve those claims.

3. Attach the information that makes the sample reviewable.

Ask for the model-specific product record, applicable IFU or instruction draft, pack-out information and a document checklist appropriate to your market discussion. If a value appears only in an image, label it as source-derived and request final confirmation.

This gives technical, commercial and quality colleagues the same reference point when they examine the sample and decide what should happen next.

4. Log feedback by decision category.

Structure feedback around product configuration, documentation gaps, packaging/brand questions and commercial next steps. Avoid a vague “good / bad” conclusion. A useful sample review gives each question an owner and identifies whether the answer requires a specification update, a document request, an OEM feasibility conversation or a different model.

5. Choose the next procurement decision.

After a controlled sample review, the next step may be a distributor document review, an OEM brief, a packaging discussion or a commercial quotation based on an agreed configuration. Keeping that sequence clear prevents a prototype or preliminary sample from becoming the basis for an unsupported public promise.

Open a sample evaluation route.

State the model, country, buyer role and the questions your team needs to resolve. The enquiry form will route the request as a sample evaluation discussion.
