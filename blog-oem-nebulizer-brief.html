What to Include in a Portable Nebulizer OEM Brief

An OEM enquiry becomes productive when it explains the product, market, brand and document questions that have to be resolved together. A logo request alone is not a project brief.

Key takeaways

  • Start with the commercial route and target country, not just a visual identity.
  • Confirm whether the project is based on a listed model, a changed pack-out or a more complex configuration request.
  • Keep packaging, labels, documents and website claims aligned with the final product record.

1. Explain where and how the product will be sold.

The first part of an OEM brief is the target market and channel: distributor portfolio, retail brand, clinic procurement programme or another B2B route. This information changes the questions around labels, instructions, packaging, documents and public content. It also makes clear who will evaluate the next materials.

Do not ask a supplier to promise “global certification” or an all-market launch from a one-line enquiry. A responsible project scope is built country by country, model by model and document category by document category.

2. Name the product reference and the configuration question.

State the exact model if you know it. If you are evaluating ZY018G-I or ZY018G-I-2, keep the references separate until the official differences are documented. Then describe whether the project is standard supply, packaging adaptation, private label or a request that affects the configuration itself.

This helps prevent brand discussions from getting ahead of the product file. It also reduces the risk of assuming accessories, compatible liquid information, technical parameters or labels that do not apply to the final configuration.

3. Bring a practical brand and packaging input.

Brand identity can be useful early, but it should be translated into procurement questions: which languages are required, what legal entity appears on the label, what market information must be included, what pack format is planned, and what information must remain controlled until documentation is confirmed.

Custom branding, packaging and manuals may be feasible only after the model, label route and documentation requirements are understood. Avoid announcing customisation scope, MOQ or timing before that feasibility review is complete.

4. List the documents your team must see.

An OEM project might need a final specification, IFU, label and packaging record, plus other evidence categories relevant to the model and market. The buyer should identify the intended review purpose rather than simply request “all certificates.” This produces a clearer exchange and protects both sides from publishing unsupported language.

5. Define how a sample leads to a programme decision.

A sample discussion is most useful when it identifies the exact product configuration, the questions to be evaluated, the information supplied with the sample, feedback owner and the next commercial decision. It can support feasibility; it is not a substitute for a final approved technical or market file.
